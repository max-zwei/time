# Reflection
I started this module as a side quest to the module "Data Science Basics". Meaning I wanted to find data which I could analyse and play with to reach the learning objectives of Data Science Basics. Since my major is "Digital Design & Innovation" it felt right to look at the visual outcome of the data analysis and taking the module "Infographics". 

Once the first learning units were in the books, I realised the bigger potential of the learning objectives and was very keen to understand the underlying theories of why infographics are the best way to communicate a message. 
With this new twist I decided to not only create an infographic but to accompany it with a small meta study on infographics themselves. 

So far so good, now looking back the toughest phase started, taking my outcomes of the data analysis (/code) and building a story from it. At first I wasn't happy at all with the outcome of the data analysis due to the marginal changes over the four decades. I was hoping for a story like "We spent 4 hours on our phones nowadays and in 1990s we spent those 4 hours playing with our friends outside.". Not that this might not be true but the underlying data had a study format that made it hard to derive this story. The format of the study is a daily survey for the participants to jot down their activities of the day. As you might imagine yourself, you would probably put down "eating, 19:30 - 20:00" and not "social media, 19:30 - 20:00", even though you spent your 30 minutes eating on your social media feed. So there was a gap between what your screen time might say and what the study says. 

I decided to go with the study due to its scientific origin and due to the availability of insights all the way back to 1990s. After taking this speed bump, the story unfolded and I was able to go through the obstacle course of designing both the digital and print versions of my deliverables. 

My biggest learning next to the concept of infographics is having worked a lot more with the graphic design tool Affinity. I heavily use Figma for any design-related project, so it was a good excuse to leave this familiar tool landscape and try Affinity for a change. I somehow was able to find my way and with that surely lower my anxiety of trying it for a next project, but I didn't invest much in properly learning the tool and the ins-and-outs of graphic design, meaning functions like "Auto Layout" or the more simplistic UI of Figma created a few headaches.

To Figma, this was the first project where I tried the new Motion functionality in combination with Figma's Make (regarding my use of generative AI, see BRIEF.md). As with most of the functions in Figma, it was very easy to get a hang of it and allowed me to create the dynamic stories I had imagined without having to trust a one-shot LLM prompt to follow my design and intention.

# Self-assessment
The standard I set to myself is to reach Level 3 for all the design-related modules I'm taking. Infographics is no exception here, so I have to be ignorant and confident enough to therefore assess myself with a Level 3. Looking at each individual level:

**Level 0 & Level 1**
I've dove into the underlying ideas of infographics, sharpened my eye for design with evaluating infographics as well as creating a moodboard for my own infographic. I've used a suitable data visulation and shaped a design technique to communicate the data. I've taken decisions along the way based on my desired objectives and the evaluations I received. I've defined my target audience and acknowledged their requirements and expectations. I've delivered the expected outcomes (/0_deliverables).

**Level 2**
Probably the weakest demonstration among the levels as due to my picked design direction a for example deep dive into data visualation wouldn't have helped my objective. Nonetheless, I see my gained expertise in the field of the underlying approach of infographics. 
Meaning the delivery of the plaque as meta study to infographics and an answer to "Why even bother with an infographic?". 
From this research and my target audience I derived the deliberate, strictly-followed and attention-directed design of the poster. Meaning to pick a design technique suiting their unique attention span and focusing on the same attention patterns as social media, mixing short and simple data points with short and simple descriptions and the visual hooks of memes. 
Again due to my target audience I decided to go further with the deliverables "Long scroll" and "Teaser" to more sophisticated versions, acknowledging that my target audience rarely uses a poster or even prints in general as their primary medium in daily life. This resulted in the endless scroll web version of the "Long scroll" and the dynamic social media stories for the "Teaser".

**Level 3**
The first attempt at this level, so I'm not a 100% sure if my level of publishing achieves it. 
I've created a Behance project (https://www.behance.net/gallery/257763123/Scrolling-through-time-Social-life-and-media-habits) which I additionaly shared on LinkedIn (https://lnkd.in/p/exBTp3QC) with an accompanying post. Next to it, I published it to my portfolio website in my style of presenting a project (https://zwei.berlin/projects/time) while using similar text content as with the Behance project.

All in all, going through the level requirements I therefore assess myself with a level 3, that was my standard to begin with and looking at the outcome, I fulfilled my personal expectations.
